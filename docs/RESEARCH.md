# Audio Deepfake Detection: Research & Architectures

This document summarizes the state-of-the-art research for audio deepfake and anti-spoofing detection, serving as the architectural foundation for the OffScript 30-Phase Refactor.

## 1. AASIST (Audio Anti-Spoofing using Integrated Spectro-Temporal Graph Attention Networks)
- **Project:** [AASIST](https://github.com/clovaai/aasist)
- **Architecture:** Graph Attention Networks (GATs) applied to raw audio representations. It uses a heterogeneous graph to model artifacts in both spectral and temporal domains.
- **Training Approach:** Trained primarily on ASVspoof 2019 LA.
- **Important Findings:** High robustness against unseen attacks compared to CNN-only architectures because it captures complex relational artifacts in synthetic speech.
- **How We Use It:** Will serve as Model A in our ensemble. AASIST provides a fundamentally different inductive bias (graph structure) than standard transformers or CNNs.

## 2. RawNet2
- **Project:** [RawNet2 for Anti-Spoofing](https://github.com/clovaai/rawnet2)
- **Architecture:** End-to-end waveform processing. Uses a Sinc-convolution layer as the first layer to learn customized band-pass filters directly from raw audio, followed by Residual blocks and GRUs.
- **Training Approach:** Trained using focal loss / BCE on ASVspoof datasets.
- **Important Findings:** Operating directly on the raw waveform allows RawNet2 to detect phase anomalies and micro-artifacts that are often destroyed or obscured by STFT/Mel-spectrogram extraction.
- **How We Use It:** Will serve as Model B in our ensemble. It acts as our phase-sensitive, low-level artifact detector.

## 3. Spectral Forensics (LFCC / CQCC with LCNN)
- **Project:** Standard ASVspoof Baselines (e.g., LCNNs)
- **Architecture:** Extracts Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC) or Constant Q Cepstral Coefficients (CQCC), then passes them through a Light CNN (LCNN) utilizing Max-Feature-Map (MFM) activation.
- **Training Approach:** Standard cross-entropy.
- **Important Findings:** LFCC is particularly sensitive to high-frequency artifacts introduced by neural vocoders (like HiFi-GAN or WaveGlow).
- **How We Use It:** Will serve as Model C in our ensemble. It provides classical, interpretable spectral features that compliment deep representations.

## 4. Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Models (Wav2Vec2 / WavLM / HuBERT)
- **Project:** Various (e.g., WavLM-Large for Anti-Spoofing)
- **Architecture:** Pre-trained self-supervised transformers fine-tuned for sequence classification.
- **Training Approach:** Massive pre-training on unlabeled speech, fine-tuned with a classification head on ASVspoof or WaveFake.
- **Important Findings:** SSL models have incredibly rich phonetic and acoustic representations but are prone to catastrophic overfitting on dataset-specific acoustic conditions (e.g., room noise) rather than the spoofing artifact itself unless carefully regularized (e.g., via Layer-wise learning rate decay or LoRA).
- **How We Use It:** Will serve as Model D. We will use a Wav2Vec2/WavLM checkpoint, fine-tuning the classification head while keeping the feature extractor largely frozen or using LoRA to prevent overfitting.

## Datasets

### ASVspoof 2019 / 2021
- **Domain:** Logical Access (LA) and Deepfake (DF).
- **Limitations:** Many models overfit to the specific codecs and channel effects in ASVspoof.
- **Usage:** Primary training/validation split.

### WaveFake
- **Domain:** English and Japanese generated by 6 different neural vocoders (MelGAN, Parallel WaveGAN, Multi-band MelGAN, Full-band MelGAN, HiFi-GAN, WaveGlow).
- **Usage:** Secondary training set to increase vocoder variance and unseen-generator robustness.

### Fake-or-Real (FoR)
- **Domain:** In-the-wild deepfakes (politicians, celebrities) mixed with real audio.
- **Usage:** Out-of-distribution (OOD) testing to ensure the model generalizes beyond sterile acoustic environments.
